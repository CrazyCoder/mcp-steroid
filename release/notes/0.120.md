## 0.120

`steroid_ui` pictures reach past Settings: code lines, the point of a right click with its context menu, an inspection deep in the Inspections tree, refactoring dialogs, and the tabs of run and debug tool windows. A screenshot reports what it shows cut, such as a pane squeezed by a splitter or a column header in a narrow dialog, with the step that makes room, and `"fit": true` runs those steps first. A new `splitter` step moves a splitter's divider, and a snapshot lists each splitter's state.

### Code and the click point

![The editor's context menu opened by a right click, with the click point and the Refactor item marked](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CrazyCoder/mcp-steroid/v0.120/release/notes/img/0.120-context-menu.png)

The picture above is one `steroid_ui` call: a `goto` to a symbol, a right `click` on the editor, then a `screenshot` with `"highlight":[{"click":true,"label":"right-click"},{"text":"Refactor"}]` and `"crop":"popups"`, with `"restore": true`.

- A highlight takes `{"lines":"20-27","file":"src/a.ts"}` or `{"symbol":"parse"}` of code. The outline follows the lines as the editor paints them, wrapped lines and folds included. `"crop":"highlights"` keeps the line numbers, and the caret is hidden while the picture paints.
- A `click` on an editor lands at its caret, or at `line` and `column` or a `symbol`, so a right click opens the context menu at the code. `{"click":true}` marks where the last click went with a mouse pointer.
- `{"console":"App","contains":"Exception"}` outlines the last console line of a run that holds the text, in a console built on an editor or on a terminal.
- Highlights are numbered 1, 2, 3 when there are several, as steps to follow. A single highlight is outlined without a number, and `"numbers": true` or `false` chooses.
- A badge and its label keep off the text of other controls, such as the neighbouring tabs of a tab row or a menu opened by the click. With no free spot they take the one that covers the least text.

### Trees, tabs and inspections

- `{"inspection":"NullableProblems"}` outlines an inspection's row on the Settings Inspections page, its groups expanded, and `{"action":"select","inspection":"NullableProblems"}` selects it. Both work from a JetBrains Client in Split Mode.
- A tree table, such as the Inspections tree, reads, selects and highlights its rows by path, `Java > Probable bugs > Nullability problems`, as a tree does.
- The inner tabs of a run or debug session, such as Console and Threads & Variables, are rows of their tab bar: `select` switches them and a highlight outlines one. `"crop":{"toolwindow":"Debug"}` cuts a picture to a tool window.
- A `run` step of a run, debug or stop action says which runs it started or stopped, or that it started none, as a configuration that already runs and allows one instance does.

### Cut content and splitters

- A snapshot ends with a `layout:` line for content cut inside panes, fields and headers: rows a splitter pane squeezes, a tree cut at the right, the text of a field, a combo box or a table header in a dialog, a truncated label. Each line names the step that makes room: a `splitter` step, a tool window step, or a `window` step with the width a dialog needs.
- A `screenshot` report ends with a `cut:` line for each of those in the part it shows. `"fit": true` runs the steps first, and the restore puts the sizes back. In Split Mode the lines cover what the JetBrains Client draws; the controls of a host Settings page give none.
- `{"action":"splitter","ref":"e40","proportion":0.3}` gives a splitter's first pane that share, `"size":240` gives the pane that holds the target that many pixels, and `"size":"fit"` gives it the room its content lacks, never shrinking it. It works on the IDE's splitters, such as Project Structure's, the debugger's panes and editor splits, and on Swing's split panes. The restore puts the divider back, and the proportion the IDE saves for the next opening.
- A snapshot lists each splitter with its state, such as `OnePixelSplitter [ref=e40] horizontal 0.25`.

### Fixes

- The rows of a table drawn by an editor-based renderer, such as Kotlin's Change Signature parameters, each read their own text. Before, every row read as the row painted last.
- An empty editor-based table cell reads as empty, not as the name of the code fragment behind it.
- A `select` or `scroll` by a tree path names the row by its path in its report whether or not its parents were already expanded.
- Console text in reports carries no ANSI color codes.
- The part of a picture that popups reach past the window takes the window's background. Before, it stayed transparent, which a JPEG saves as black.
- A snapshot leaves out table cells that paint only an icon, and blank cells.
